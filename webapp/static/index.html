Connecting your microscope

  • USB microscope camera: plug it into this computer, then pick it from the camera menu at the top.
  • Phone on an eyepiece adapter: use a webcam app (e.g. Continuity Camera on Mac, or a phone-as-webcam app) so it shows up as a camera.
  • Microscope with HDMI out: connect it through a USB HDMI capture adapter.
  • Close the microscope's own software first, since only one app can use a camera at a time.
  • Focus sharply and keep the slide still for a second for the steadiest count.

White cell differential

How it works

About this project

This app was built as part of a master's research project. It is for education and research only and is not a medical device. AI can make mistakes: counts and white cell types may be wrong, and nothing here is a diagnosis. Always consult a qualified healthcare provider or a certified laboratory for medical advice and an accurate diagnosis.

What it counts

  • Red blood cells (RBC): carry oxygen; the most common cell in a smear.
  • White blood cells (WBC): immune cells; larger, with a dark purple nucleus.
  • Platelets: small fragments that help blood clot.

White cell types

A second AI model looks at each white blood cell the counter finds and names its type: neutrophil, lymphocyte, monocyte, eosinophil, basophil or immature granulocyte. The breakdown (a "WBC differential") shows how many of each and their share. Hover a white cell to see the model's top three guesses. Switch the toolbar to White cells (or press W) to hide red cells and platelets and put the white cell breakdown first. Typing a few cells gives a rough picture; a reliable differential needs many cells (often 100+), e.g. by capturing several views in live mode.

Guided white cell differential

In Live camera, choose 50, 100 or 200 cells and press Start counting, then move the slide slowly across new areas. The app measures how the slide moves between frames, so each white cell is counted once even when it stays in view. A cell counts after it is seen in two frames, and its type is a vote over every frame it appeared in. The finished report lists each type with a 95% range (how much the percentage could vary by chance) and typical adult ranges for context, and can be saved as CSV or PDF. If you move too fast the app pauses counting and asks you to slow down; going back over areas you've already counted after tracking is lost may count some cells twice.

Strictness

Each cell the AI finds comes with a certainty score. Relaxed counts more cells but may include a few mistakes, Strict only counts cells it's very sure about, and Balanced is a good default. Changing it updates the counts instantly.

Tips for better counts

  • Use a sharp, well-lit photo taken through the microscope.
  • Images similar to the samples (stained smear, cells clearly visible) work best.
  • Cells cut off at the edge of the image may be missed or counted twice in overlapping photos.
  • Hover a box to see its certainty; use zoom to check small platelets.

Live camera

Press Live camera to count straight from a microscope camera (USB, HDMI capture adapter, or a phone on an eyepiece adapter). The app counts several frames a second. Pause freezes a frame for a closer look, and Capture saves it to your images.

Privacy

Images you upload are analyzed on this computer and are not saved. Links are downloaded by this app from the website you give.

About the model

YOLO26s detector fine-tuned on the TXL-PBC dataset. On held-out test images it found 95% of cells, and 97% of its detections were correct.
